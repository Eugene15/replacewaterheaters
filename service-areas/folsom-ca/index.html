Storage tank

A cylindrical tank that stores hot water. Note whether it uses gas or electricity and whether the replacement stays in the same location.

Tankless

A wall-mounted unit that heats water on demand. Existing tankless replacement and conversion from a tank involve different questions.

Heat pump

A high-efficiency electric unit that uses surrounding air. Space, airflow, condensate drainage and electrical capacity matter.

Type, capacity, fuel source and efficiency level establish the starting point.

The connections

Access and removal

Keep the type or change it?

Compare the work around the equipment, not only the equipment itself.

Usually the most direct path

Replace like for like

A compatible unit using the same fuel source and location may limit the number of installation variables.

  • Current hot-water capacity works
  • Existing location remains practical
  • No larger equipment conversion is wanted

Compact, with more variables

Switch to tankless

Tankless heats water on demand, but conversion from a storage tank may involve more than mounting a different appliance.

  • Gas supply or electrical capacity
  • Venting, power and drainage
  • Water connections and clearances

Efficient electric alternative

Consider heat pump

A heat-pump water heater may be worth comparing when the installation area can support the system.

  • Room volume and airflow
  • Condensate drainage
  • Electrical circuit and clearances

Folsom permit starting point

The city lists water heater changeouts among its minor permits.

Folsom’s ePermit Center identifies water heater changeouts as work handled through the city’s minor-permit process. The requirements still need to be confirmed for the exact address and proposed scope before installation.

A compatible replacement and a conversion to a different system type may raise different site questions, so decide what you want to compare before relying on general permit information.

Official source: Folsom ePermit Center.
