Heat-pump readiness scorecard

Five site conditions shape the decision.

A heat-pump assessment starts with consequences for the actual building. Electrical capacity determines whether new circuit or panel work needs review. Room size and airflow affect whether the equipment can operate in the proposed location. Condensate needs a viable route, and operating sound should be considered near bedrooms or shared spaces. Stairs, doors, common areas, and shutoff coordination can also shape the installation plan.

Staying with the existing system type remains a valid comparison. Tankless equipment introduces a different group of fuel or power, venting, drainage, flow-rate, mounting, and service-clearance questions. The goal is to identify which path deserves a detailed review—not to recommend electrification before the site information is known.

Which trade permits may apply in San Francisco?

San Francisco issues plumbing and mechanical trade permits. City analysis says heat-pump projects may involve electrical, plumbing or mechanical, and building permits, depending on scope.

Are instant online trade permits available?

City materials describe instant online trade permits for qualifying work. Eligibility depends on the actual project and should be confirmed before scheduling.

What should be checked for a heat-pump model?

Check electrical capacity, room volume and airflow, condensate routing, noise location, clearances, access, and any shared-building coordination.
